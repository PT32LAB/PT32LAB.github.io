#### 1. Practical Action Bolivia

- **Website:** [practicalaction.org/bolivia](https://practicalaction.org/where-we-work/latin-america/bolivia/) | [latam.practicalaction.org/bolivia](https://latam.practicalaction.org/bolivia/)
- **Office:** Presbitero Medina St. 2922, Sopocachi, La Paz | Phone: +595 (2) 2119345
- **What they do in Bolivia:** Two core programs — (1) Food Systems: regenerative agriculture for smallholder farmers, and (2) Climate Resilience: nature-based solutions, early warning systems, watershed management. Reached 29,000 people in 2023. Delivered 365% productivity boost through renewable energy for 4,300 farmers. Target: 20,000 farmers in regenerative practices by 2025.
- **Current projects:** Flemish-funded project (2025-2026) strengthening livelihoods and forest conservation in Tsimane-Mosetene territory; Z Zurich Foundation project (2024-2027) building climate resilience against floods, fires, and heat waves.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** Practical Action's entire mission is appropriate technology for rural communities. BARK's open-source approach to off-grid infrastructure directly aligns. They already work in Bolivia on regenerative agriculture, renewable energy, and climate resilience — exactly BARK's sectors.
- **What BARK offers them:** A living demonstration site for integrated appropriate technology. A replicable model they could document and share across their global network. Access to open-source tech designs from the maker/hardware community that Practical Action could adapt for their other programs.
- **Funding partners:** BOS+, Darwin Initiative, GIZ, ECHO, Government of Canada, Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation (SDC), Z Zurich Foundation.

Sources: [Practical Action Bolivia](https://practicalaction.org/where-we-work/latin-america/bolivia/), [Practical Action Latin America 2025 Reflections](https://latam.practicalaction.org/practical-action-en-america-latina-reflexiones-hacia-el-2025/)

#### 2. Solar Energy International (SEI)

- **Website:** [solarenergy.org](https://www.solarenergy.org/)
- **Headquarters:** Paonia, Colorado, USA
- **Latin America presence:** Opened first International Solar Training Center in Cartago, Costa Rica (partnership with CFIA and CIEMI). Has trained over 9,000 Spanish speakers since launching Spanish program in 2013. Courses available in English and Spanish.
- **What they do:** Professional solar PV installation training with hands-on facilities. Courses comply with US NEC and OSHA standards. Online courses also available.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** BARK plans solar infrastructure as a core component. SEI needs demonstration sites and training venues in South America. Bolivia's $325M rural solar electrification program creates demand for trained installers that SEI could help supply.
- **What BARK offers them:** A permanent, real-world training site in Bolivia. A community of practitioners who would take and promote their courses. Content for case studies on off-grid community solar deployment. Access to the Yungas market — a region with high solar potential and active government electrification programs.

Sources: [SEI Costa Rica Training Center](https://www.solarenergy.org/solar-energy-international-sei-opens-its-first-international-solar-training-center-in-costa-rica/), [Sol-Ark/SEI Partnership](https://sol-ark.com/about/sol-ark-cares/solar-energy-international-training-programs/)

#### 4. Habitat for Humanity Bolivia

- **Website:** [habitat.org/bolivia](https://www.habitat.org/where-we-build/bolivia)
- **Offices:** Cochabamba, La Paz, and Santa Cruz
- **Operating in Bolivia since:** 1985
- **Programs:** (1) Housing: technical assistance for self-construction, repairs, and improvements; revolving loan fund for low-income families. (2) WASH: water, sanitation, and hygiene projects targeting 76,570 people (2023-2025). - **What BARK offers them:** Innovative, documented building methods for subtropical climates. A model for community-led housing with integrated infrastructure (solar, water, sanitation). Potential volunteer hosting infrastructure.

#### 5. WWF Bolivia / WCS Bolivia / Armonia

**Note:** Three conservation organizations are active in the Yungas. Any or all could be relevant to BARK.

**WWF Bolivia:**
- **Website:** [wwf.panda.org/bolivia](https://wwf.panda.org/wwf_offices/bolivia/about/)
- Eight of WWF's Global 200 ecoregions cross into Bolivia, including the Central Andean Yungas. Bolivia signed a Project Finance for Permanence (PFP) memorandum of understanding for long-term conservation funding.
- **Yungas relevance:** The Yungas is listed as one of the most threatened and biodiverse areas in Bolivia. WWF prioritizes sustainable farming of cash crops and education to reduce overharvesting.

**WCS Bolivia (Wildlife Conservation Society):**
- **Website:** [bolivia.wcs.org](https://bolivia.wcs.org/en-us/)
- Works in Madidi, Pilon Lajas, Apolobamba, and Kaa Iya national parks in coordination with SERNAP. Ran the 18-month Identidad Madidi scientific expedition exploring Yungas paramo, Polylepis forests, and high mountain puna.
- Launched an Indigenous-Led Fund (with FUNDESNAP) channeling conservation funds to Tacana, Tsimane-Mosetene, Lecos de Apolo, and Uchupiamonas indigenous organizations.

**Armonia (BirdLife International partner):**
- **Website:** [armoniabolivia.org](https://armoniabolivia.org/)
- Bolivia's BirdLife International partner. Conserva Aves initiative: 9 projects covering 955,242 hectares across La Paz and other departments. Research on Yungas-endemic species: Bolivian Recurvebill, Yungas Antwren.

- **Why any would partner with BARK:** BARK's location in the Yungas biodiversity hotspot means its land management practices directly affect conservation outcomes. These organizations need community partners who practice sustainable land use. BARK's agroforestry and permaculture approach could be a conservation model.
- **What BARK offers them:** A community committed to biodiversity-compatible agriculture. Monitoring data from community land (bird counts, species surveys). A sustainable tourism base for eco-tourism and bird-watching (Yungas has extraordinary bird diversity). Land buffer zones managed with conservation principles.

Sources: [WWF Bolivia](https://www.worldwildlife.org/news/stories/a-commitment-to-nature-in-bolivia/), [WCS Madidi](https://newsroom.wcs.org/News-Releases/articleType/ArticleView/articleId/6755/), [WCS Indigenous Fund](https://newsroom.wcs.org/News-Releases/articleType/ArticleView/articleId/21676/), [Armonia Conserva Aves](https://dev.armoniabolivia.org/conserva-aves-bolivia-nine-projects-under-implementation-covering-nearly-1000000-hectares-of-protected-areas/)

#### 6. GIZ (Deutsche Gesellschaft fur Internationale Zusammenarbeit)

- **Website:** [giz.de/bolivia](https://www.giz.de/en/regions/latin-america-caribbean/bolivia)
- **Office:** La Paz (since 1995; working in Bolivia since 1962)
- **Commissioning party:** German Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ)
- **Key programs:**
  - **Water and sanitation:** Advisory services and training for water utilities. Over 2 million Bolivians have benefited from improved drinking water and sanitation.
  - **Rural development:** Resource-saving irrigation systems improving conditions for 200,000+ rural people.
  - **Innovation Fund:** BMZ-commissioned fund supporting innovative projects promoting rule of law, human rights, and governance.
  - **Triangular cooperation:** Mexico-Bolivia-Germany program on wastewater reuse for agricultural irrigation.
  - **Dual vocational training:** Promoting German-model apprenticeship programs.
  - **Climate:** GIZ is implementing a GCF readiness project in Bolivia (approved April 2025) for strengthening national adaptation planning.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** GIZ's water, sanitation, and irrigation programs directly match BARK's infrastructure needs. Their vocational training model aligns with BARK's education/incubator vision. They are already a funding partner of Practical Action in Bolivia.
- **What BARK offers them:** A showcase project combining multiple GIZ focus areas (water, agriculture, renewable energy, vocational training) in one site. German development audiences love integrated community models.

Sources: [GIZ Bolivia Overview](https://www.giz.de/en/regions/latin-america-caribbean/bolivia), [GIZ Innovation Fund](https://www.giz.de/en/projects/innovation-fund-bolivia), [GIZ Triangular Cooperation](https://www.giz.de/en/projects/triangular-cooperation-latin-america-and-caribbean)

#### 7. USAID Bolivia — Status Warning

- **Website:** [usaid.gov/bolivia](https://usaid.gov/stabilization-and-transitions/closed-programs/bolivia)
- **Historical funding:** ~$85M/year in development assistance
- **CRITICAL UPDATE:** USAID was expelled from Bolivia in 2013. However, in late 2025, new Bolivian president Rodrigo Paz Pereira restored relations with the US at ambassador level. In February 2026, Bolivia revived anti-drug alliance with the DEA.
- **HOWEVER:** The Trump administration ended 83% of USAID projects globally in 2025, and USAID is scheduled to close by September 2026. This makes USAID an unreliable partner for any new project.
- **Assessment:** Do not plan around USAID funding. The bilateral relationship is improving but the agency itself is being dismantled. Monitor whether successor programs emerge.

Sources: [USAID Bolivia Closed Programs](https://usaid.gov/stabilization-and-transitions/closed-programs/bolivia), [Bolivia-US Relations Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia%E2%80%93United_States_relations), [Donor Tracker: USAID Closure](https://donortracker.org/policy_updates?policy=us-government-announces-official-closure-of-usaid-2025)

#### 8. Permaculture and Regenerative Agriculture Organizations in Bolivia

**Sustainable Bolivia / Fundacion Amazonia:**
- **Website:** [sustainablebolivia.org](https://sustainablebolivia.org/) | [pisatahua.org/sustainable-bolivia](https://pisatahua.org/sustainable-bolivia/)
- Founded 2007 in Cochabamba. International arm of Fundacion Amazonia (founded 2017). 1,000+ volunteers from 50+ countries. Raised ~$600K for grassroots initiatives. Established the 20,000+ hectare Aquicuana Reserve. Programs: ecotourism, environmental education, reforestation, regenerative agriculture, conservation.
- **Partnership angle:** Direct organizational model overlap with BARK. Could share operational knowledge, volunteer networks, and donor contacts.

**Kaiwai Permaculture Bolivia:**
- **Website:** [kaiwaipermaculturebolivia.com](https://www.kaiwaipermaculturebolivia.com)
- Located in Konchupata, Mizque, Cochabamba (not Yungas). Run by Certified Permaculture Designer Simon since 2014. Offers Holistic Living Permaculture courses ($450/month including meals and accommodation). Topics: organic gardening, watershed restoration, soil regeneration, companion planting, animal integration.
- **Partnership angle:** Knowledge exchange and cross-referral. Different region but same philosophy. Could co-develop curriculum.

**Centro Sara Ana:**
- International training center in Bolivia. 1,000+ visitors annually including farmers, researchers, students, and political authorities. Focus: regenerative agriculture, agroforestry systems, ecological research.
- **Partnership angle:** Established training infrastructure that BARK could learn from or partner with on joint programs.

**PRIAS Project (Conservation Strategy Fund):**
- **Website:** [conservation-strategy.org/project/prias-i](https://www.conservation-strategy.org/project/promoting-regenerative-agriculture-practices-bolivia-prias-i)
- Funded by Land Innovation Fund. Partners: FCBC, CREA, CSF. Duration: May 2023-April 2025 (completed). 30-40 farms, ~1,000 hectares. Compared regenerative vs conventional soy/cattle production. Located in Chiquitano, Cerrado, Chaco, and Amazon ecoregions.
- **Partnership angle:** Their data proving regenerative agriculture profitability strengthens BARK's investment case. Could apply similar methodology to Yungas coffee/cacao.

**FiBL / SysCom Bolivia (Cocoa Research):**
- **Website:** [systems-comparison.fibl.org/bolivia](https://systems-comparison.fibl.org/project-sites/bolivia.html)
- Swiss Research Institute of Organic Agriculture (FiBL) has run a 15-year long-term cocoa farming systems comparison in Bolivia since 2008/2009. Partners: Piaf El Ceibo, Ecotop, Institute of Ecology at UMSA. Compares organic vs conventional in monoculture vs agroforestry systems.
- **Key finding:** 15 years of data showing competitive yields in organic agroforestry cocoa. Published in Agronomy for Sustainable Development (2025).
- **Partnership angle:** Direct scientific backing for BARK's cacao agroforestry plans. Potential for BARK to become a research site or adopt their proven methods.

**Ecosystem Restoration Camps:**
- Active in Bolivia. Approaches include regenerative agriculture, agroecology, agroforestry, permaculture, and conservation agriculture.
- **Partnership angle:** Could coordinate volunteer restoration work at BARK site.

Sources: [Sustainable Bolivia](https://pisatahua.org/sustainable-bolivia/), [Kaiwai Permaculture](https://www.kaiwaipermaculturebolivia.com), [PRIAS Project](https://www.conservation-strategy.org/project/promoting-regenerative-agriculture-practices-bolivia-prias-i), [FiBL SysCom Bolivia](https://systems-comparison.fibl.org/project-sites/bolivia.html), [FiBL 15-year results](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13593-025-01073-8)

### Tech / Open Source Organizations

#### 9. Open Source Ecology (OSE)

- **Website:** [opensourceecology.org](https://www.opensourceecology.org/)
- **What they do:** Developing the Global Village Construction Set (GVCS) — 50 open-source industrial machines needed to build a small civilization with modern comforts. All designs released under GNU General Public License. Main prototyping at Factor e Farm, Missouri, USA. OSE Germany operates independently.
- **Current status:** Some machines well-developed (compressed earth brick press, tractor, 3D printer), others still in prototype or concept phase. Community of farmers, engineers, and architects contributing designs.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** BARK is literally the use case OSE was designed for — an off-grid community building its own infrastructure. A Yungas deployment would be OSE's most significant international implementation. BARK could test and iterate on GVCS machines in real tropical conditions.
- **What BARK offers them:** Real-world field testing in a developing-world context (their designs are primarily tested in US conditions). Documentation of machine performance in tropical/subtropical climate. A community of technically skilled users who can contribute improvements back to the project. Proof of concept for the GVCS vision outside the US.

Source: [Open Source Ecology](https://www.opensourceecology.org/), [GVCS Overview](https://wiki.opensourceecology.org/wiki/Global_Village_Construction_Set)

#### 11. Precious Plastic

- **Website:** [preciousplastic.com](https://www.preciousplastic.com/) | [Community Map](https://community.preciousplastic.com/map)
- **What they do:** Open-source recycling machine designs, downloadable starter kits for creating recycling businesses (Community Points, Collection Points, machine shops). 80,000-strong global community. Machines built from widely available, low-cost components.
- **Latin America presence:** Active interest and growing community in Chile, Brazil, and Argentina. Workspace set up in Chile (2018) in partnership with Karun, focused on women's employment.
- **Bolivia-specific presence:** No confirmed Precious Plastic operation in Bolivia found in search results. This is an opportunity, not a gap.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** Plastic waste is a growing problem in Bolivian rural communities. BARK could become Bolivia's first or among the first Precious Plastic Community Points. The open-source ethos is identical.
- **What BARK offers them:** A new market entry point in Bolivia. Integration with a community that has the technical skills to build and operate the machines. A model for how plastic recycling integrates with other community infrastructure.

Sources: [Precious Plastic Universe](https://www.preciousplastic.com/), [MIT Solve: Precious Plastic](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/15640)

#### 12. RepRap / Arduino / Maker Communities in Latin America

- **RepRap:** Open-source self-replicating 3D printer. Materials cost ~EUR 350. Licensed under GNU GPL. Designed explicitly to democratize manufacturing for communities in the developing world.
- **Latin American maker movement:** Growing across Chile, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia, and Paraguay. In Latin America, maker culture developed partly by necessity — people learn to improvise solutions and extend product life. New hackerspaces and makerspaces appearing across the region.
- **Bolivia-specific:** 3D printing companies are gaining ground in Bolivia, but no specific Bolivian RepRap community or hackerspace was found in search results. The Fab Labs listed above (section 10) are the closest existing infrastructure.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** BARK's vision of local manufacturing from open-source designs is the RepRap philosophy applied at community scale. 3D printing enables production of spare parts, tools, and components without supply chain dependency.
- **What BARK offers them:** A purpose-driven deployment site. Real use cases for 3D-printed parts in off-grid infrastructure. Documentation of what works and what doesn't in tropical conditions. Potential for a permanent makerspace that advances the Latin American maker movement.

Sources: [RepRap Project](https://reprap.org/), [Latin America 3D Printing Revolution](https://www.3dnatives.com/en/latin-americas-3d-printing-revolution-200120216/), [Maker Movement in Latin America (Make Magazine)](https://makezine.com/article/maker-news/the-maker-movement-in-latin-america-an-interview-with-tiburcio-de-la-carcova/)

### Academic Partners

#### 13. Unidad Academica Campesina - Carmen Pampa (UAC-CP)

- **Website:** [carmenpampafund.org](https://carmenpampafund.org/)
- **Location:** Carmen Pampa, Nor Yungas, Bolivia — approximately 12 km from Coroico
- **Affiliation:** Operates under partnership with Catholic University of Bolivia (La Paz), though receives no funding from them. Joint effort of the Catholic University, Missionary Franciscan Sisters (MFIC), Diocese of Coroico, and Sub-Central Villa Nilo (local indigenous governing body).
- **Programs:**
  - **Agronomy** (5-year): sustainable agriculture, model vegetable garden, compost center, plant nursery, coffee production. All graduates defend a research project or business plan.
  - **Veterinary Science** (5-year): animal production, meat processing, beekeeping.
  - **Nursing** (5-year): public health specialization, practical experience in La Paz hospitals and rural clinics.
  - **Education** (5-year): alternative, special, and administrative education specializations.
  - **Ecotourism** (licenciatura since 2013): sustainable tourism, national parks, cultural heritage.
  - **Pre-University Program:** ~80 students/semester for academic preparation.
  - **Food Cooperative:** Provides subsidized meals to 70% of students.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** They are 12 km away. Their programs in agronomy, ecotourism, and veterinary science directly overlap with BARK's needs. Their students need practicum sites and thesis projects. The coffee production program is directly relevant.
- **What BARK offers them:** Internship and practicum placements for students. Real-world research sites for thesis projects. Guest lectures and workshops from BARK's technical specialists. Potential joint courses in appropriate technology, permaculture, or cooperative business.
- **Key consideration:** Carmen Pampa lost USAID funding when USAID was expelled in 2013. The Carmen Pampa Fund (US-based) now handles fundraising and volunteer recruitment. They actively seek international partnerships.

Sources: [Carmen Pampa Fund Programs](https://carmenpampafund.org/programs/), [UAC-Carmen Pampa Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidad_Acad%C3%A9mica_Campesina-Carmen_Pampa), [Carmen Pampa Fund](https://carmenpampafund.org/)

### Cooperative Networks

#### 16. CASA Latina (Council of Sustainable Settlements of Latin America)

- **Website:** [ecovillage.org/region/casa](https://ecovillage.org/region/casa/)
- **Part of:** Global Ecovillage Network (GEN)
- **Founded:** 2012, formally constituted at the Call of the Mountain in Atlantida Ecovillage, Colombia.
- **Scope:** 100+ sustainable settlements across Latin America. 4 consolidated national networks (Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico). 2 thematic networks (Nomads, Youth). 5 emergent networks (Argentina, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Peru, Uruguay). Held 3 general assemblies in 2025 (one in-person in Misiones, Argentina; two virtual).
- **Key distinction:** CASA Latina is not just intentional ecovillages — it consciously includes traditional communities, Indigenous territories, Afro-descendant councils, rural cooperatives, and urban regenerative projects.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** BARK is exactly the kind of project CASA Latina exists to connect and support. Membership would give BARK immediate access to a network of 100+ similar communities, shared knowledge, and collective advocacy.
- **What BARK offers them:** A new node in the network, particularly in an underrepresented area (Bolivia). A model integrating technology with sustainability (many CASA communities are low-tech). Open-source documentation that other CASA members could replicate.

Sources: [CASA Latina - GEN](https://ecovillage.org/region/casa/), [CASA Latina Lives On](https://ecovillage.org/casa-latina-lives-on/), [CASA Weaving Resilience](https://www.gaiaeducation.org/blog/117179-casa-latina-weaving-resilience-across-a)

#### 17. Cooperatives of the Americas (ICA Regional Office)

- **Website:** [aciamericas.coop](https://aciamericas.coop/en/)
- **Regional office:** San Jose, Costa Rica (established 1990)
- **Mission:** Repositioning the cooperative model; promoting business development and human resource development across the Americas.
- **Bolivia context:** Bolivia's State Political Constitution recognizes the cooperative sector in 12 articles. The cooperative tradition is rooted in ancestral Andean practices: ayni (reciprocity) and ayllu (community production). Bolivia has 15,000 active cooperatives regulated by AFCOOP.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** BARK as a cooperative provides a modern, tech-integrated model that ICA could promote as innovation in the cooperative movement. The combination of traditional Andean cooperative values with open-source technology is compelling.
- **What BARK offers them:** A case study in cooperative tech community building. A bridge between the maker/open-source movement and the cooperative movement. Documentation of cooperative governance for a mixed international/local membership.

Sources: [Cooperatives of the Americas - Bolivia](https://aciamericas.coop/en/paises/bolivia/), [ICA-ILO Bolivia Publication](https://coops4dev.coop/en/4devamericas/bolivia)

#### 18. Mondragon University

- **Website:** [mondragon.edu](https://www.mondragon.edu/en/)
- **Location:** Basque Country, Spain
- **Model:** Non-profit cooperative university where students, faculty, and staff are all cooperative members. Integrates work and study with industry internships.
- **International programs:** LEINN (Entrepreneurial Leadership and Innovation) — official European Bachelor's degree with international travel component ("Travelling University"). Mondragon University Mexico in Queretaro. Numerous international exchange partnerships.
- **Master's in Cooperativism and Socio-Business Management** available.
- **Why they'd partner with BARK:** Mondragon is the world's most successful cooperative ecosystem. They actively seek international partners to spread the cooperative model. BARK's cooperative structure in a developing-world context would be interesting to their researchers and students.
- **What BARK offers them:** A field study site for cooperative governance research. A LEINN student project destination. A case study for their Master's in Cooperativism program. A test of whether Mondragon principles translate to a Bolivian Yungas context.

#### 4. Cooperative Development Framework

- **Legal framework:** Ley General de Cooperativas No. 356 (April 11, 2013) + Decreto Supremo No. 1995 (May 2014, implementing regulation). Replaced the 1958 law.
- **Regulator:** AFCOOP (Autoridad de Fiscalizacion y Control de Cooperativas) — under the Ministry of Labor. Replaced the old INALCO. Regulates, audits, and supervises 15,000 active cooperatives in Bolivia.
- **Constitutional support:** Bolivia's State Political Constitution recognizes the cooperative sector in 12 articles and promotes cooperative participation.
- **Cooperative definition under law:** Non-profit association of natural and/or legal persons, voluntarily associated, founded on solidarity work and cooperation, with autonomous and democratic structure.
- **Classification:** Productive cooperatives (mining, agricultural, others), Service cooperatives (housing, education, others), Public service cooperatives (telecommunications, electricity, water).
- **Registration requirements:** Legal Personality Request Document, Act of Formation, General Assembly Call, Constitution documents, Organic Statutes, Socio-economic study, Board of Directors and Surveillance member election minutes. Must submit to AFCOOP within 90 calendar days of formation.
- **Foreign member participation:** No specific prohibition on foreign nationals found in search results, but also no explicit confirmation. This requires verification with a Bolivian cooperative law specialist or AFCOOP directly.
- **How BARK qualifies:** BARK as an agricultural/productive cooperative fits squarely within the law. The cooperative structure gives BARK legal personality, tax benefits, and government recognition.
- **Source URLs:** [Ley 356 Full Text](https://bolivia.infoleyes.com/norma/4397/ley-general-de-cooperativas-356), [AFCOOP](https://www.afcoop.gob.bo/), [ICA: New Legal Framework for Bolivian Cooperatives](https://ica.coop/en/media/news/new-legal-framework-bolivian-co-operatives), [Cooperative Registration Requirements](https://tramitalofacil.com/en/requisitos-para-crear-una-cooperativa-en-bolivia/)

## Topic 3: Investment Framework

### Why Invest in BARK

#### 1. Resilience Hedge

BARK represents portfolio diversification into **physical, productive, community-owned assets** in a geographically isolated, self-sufficient setting. In an era of increasing geopolitical instability, supply chain disruptions, and climate uncertainty, a functioning off-grid community with food production, energy independence, and local manufacturing capability is a hedge against civilization-level risks.

This is not speculative — it is the same logic behind agricultural land investment, gold holdings, and rural property acquisition, but with the added dimension of active community management and productive capacity.

#### 2. Impact Investing

BARK produces measurable social and environmental impact:
- **Social:** Education, vocational training, cooperative governance, community development in one of Bolivia's poorest regions. The Yungas has significant development needs (Carmen Pampa serves students who are among Bolivia's most marginalized).
- **Environmental:** Agroforestry replacing deforestation, renewable energy deployment, waste recycling, biodiversity conservation in a global biodiversity hotspot.
- **Economic:** Local job creation, value-added processing of raw materials (coffee, cacao), technology transfer, and market access for smallholder farmers.

Impact metrics can be structured around the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 4 (Quality Education), SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy), SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth), SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities), SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), SDG 13 (Climate Action), SDG 15 (Life on Land).

#### 3. Early-Stage Opportunity

BARK is ground floor. Early participants shape the governance, design, and culture of the community. The potential replicability of the model (open-source documentation means others can build their own) creates a network effect: each new BARK-inspired community validates the model and increases the value of the original.

#### 4. Real Asset Backing

Unlike purely financial instruments, BARK investment is backed by:
- **Land** in the Yungas (appreciating asset in a region with increasing interest from eco-tourists, retirees, and remote workers)
- **Infrastructure:** Solar arrays, water systems, processing equipment, buildings
- **Equipment:** Coffee and cacao processing lines, fabrication tools, 3D printers, recycling machines
- **Productive capacity:** Revenue-generating agricultural operations and services

#### 5. Revenue-Generating Components

BARK is not a charity. It has multiple revenue streams:
- **Coffee:** Yungas specialty coffee commands premium prices ($3-8/lb green, higher for roasted specialty)
- **Cacao:** Bolivian wild/agroforestry cacao is increasingly valued in craft chocolate markets
- **Retreats:** Technoshamanic retreat center in a world-class natural setting (Coroico already attracts tourism)
- **Solar services:** Installation, maintenance, and training for the 140,000 families targeted by Bolivia's electrification program
- **Education/training:** Permaculture courses, maker workshops, cooperative business training
- **Fabrication services:** CNC, 3D printing, and prototyping for local businesses and the startup incubator

### Investment Modalities

#### 1. Cooperative Membership Shares

**Structure under Bolivian Law (Ley 356):**
- Cooperative members contribute "certificados de aportacion" (share certificates)
- The General Assembly is the supreme authority; decisions bind all members
- Democratic governance: one member, one vote (regardless of share size)
- Board of Directors and Surveillance Committee elected by members
- Cooperatives are classified as non-profit — surplus is reinvested or distributed per cooperative statute, not as dividends in the traditional sense
- Registration through AFCOOP with 90-day window after formation

**BARK implementation:**
- Membership share buy-in at a defined tier (e.g., founding member, standard member, associate member)
- Each share grants voting rights in the General Assembly
- Surplus distribution per the cooperative's organic statute (to be drafted)
- **Unverified:** Whether foreign nationals can hold cooperative membership under Ley 356. Legal consultation required before marketing this to international investors.

**Risk note:** Cooperative shares are not liquid. There is no secondary market. Exit requires selling back to the cooperative or finding a replacement member per the statute.

#### 2. Component-Specific Funding

Investors can sponsor specific infrastructure components:

#### 3. In-Kind Contributions

- **Equipment:** Solar panels, tools, 3D printers, agricultural equipment, computers, networking hardware
- **Expertise:** Engineering design, legal services, architectural plans, permaculture design, cooperative governance consulting
- **Intellectual property:** Open-source designs, software, training curricula, documentation
- **Valuation:** In-kind contributions valued at fair market value and converted to cooperative share equivalents per a schedule approved by the General Assembly

#### 4. Residency Investment

- **Concept:** Buy-in for community membership plus a living space (constructed or self-built on allocated land)
- **Includes:** Cooperative membership share + land use allocation + community services access
- **Does NOT include:** Land ownership (land is collectively owned by the cooperative)
- **Obligation:** Residency investors commit to community participation (governance, work contributions, cultural integration)
- **Legal structure:** Use rights granted by cooperative lease agreement; reversionary clause if member exits
- **Estimated range:** $5K-$30K depending on housing tier and phase (earlier = cheaper)

**Critical legal note:** Foreign land ownership in Bolivia is restricted near borders (50 km). Coroico is approximately 65-70 km from the Peruvian border. This needs legal verification to confirm the cooperative (and its foreign members) can hold land title without restriction.

#### 5. Working Contributions (Sweat Equity)

- **Concept:** Earn cooperative shares through labor
- **Eligible work:** Construction, agricultural labor, teaching, technical skills, administration, community management
- **Valuation:** Hours tracked and converted to share value at a rate set by the General Assembly (e.g., X hours = 1 share unit)
- **Cap:** Maximum share accumulation through sweat equity to be defined (prevents concentration of governance power)
- **Verification:** Work validated by project leads; disputes resolved through Surveillance Committee

### Honest Risk Disclosure

**Illiquidity:** Cooperative shares cannot be freely traded. There is no secondary market. Exit requires cooperative approval and may involve loss of invested capital.

**Early-stage risk:** BARK does not yet exist as an operating entity. All projections are forward-looking estimates based on comparable projects and market research. Actual results may vary significantly.

**Country risk:** Bolivia is experiencing economic instability (fuel shortages, dollar scarcity). Political changes may affect investment climate positively or negatively. The legal environment for foreign investment is complex and evolving.

**Currency risk:** Revenue is generated in Bolivianos (BOB); most investors hold USD, EUR, or other currencies. Bolivia's fixed exchange rate regime has been under pressure.

**Land risk:** Collective land ownership means no individual can claim a specific parcel. Cooperative dissolution procedures are governed by Ley 356 and may not favor individual investors. Foreign land holding near borders requires legal verification.

**Operational risk:** Off-grid community infrastructure in a remote, subtropical mountain environment is inherently challenging. Supply chains, road access (landslides in rainy season), and technical maintenance are real operational risks.

**Coca factor:** The Yungas is a traditional coca-growing region. While legal, this creates regulatory scrutiny and occasional security incidents. Travel advisories reference this.

**What BARK does about risk:**
- Open-source documentation means the knowledge survives even if a specific BARK site doesn't
- Cooperative governance distributes decision-making and reduces single-point-of-failure risk
- Multiple revenue streams reduce dependence on any single income source
- Community self-sufficiency (food, energy, water) reduces external dependency
- Strong NGO and academic partnerships provide institutional support and legitimacy
