## 1. Coffee Processing (Wet Mill + Roasting)

### Context

- Yungas produces ~95% of Bolivia's coffee, almost entirely arabica
- 85--95% of growers are smallholders on 1--8 hectare plots
- Growing elevation 1,200--2,000m; shade-cultivated under forest canopy
- Bolivian arabica yields approximately 400--700 kg green beans/hectare (conservative for Yungas; Peru Amazonia comparables report 700--900 kg/ha)
- Assumption: cooperative aggregating from 5--10 hectares = 2,000--5,000 kg green beans/year

### Investment

| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Small wet mill (depulper, fermentation tanks, washing, drying beds) | $1,500--$12,000 |
| Used 5 kg roaster (e.g., Mill City, Diedrich) | $3,000--$10,000 |
| New 5--15 kg production roaster | $18,000--$40,000 |
| Packaging equipment, grinder, sealer | $2,000--$5,000 |
| Facility setup, storage | $2,000--$5,000 |
| **Total (conservative)** | **$25,000--$55,000** |

Note: A used 5 kg roaster at $3,000--$10,000 is the budget entry point. A new 15 kg roaster at $45,000--$80,000 is for scale-up phase.

### Revenue Model

**Scenario: 2,000 kg green beans processed per year (conservative)**

- Green coffee loses ~20% weight during roasting = 1,600 kg roasted
- 1,600 kg = 3,527 lbs roasted coffee

Revenue channels (blended):
- **Direct-to-consumer online (40%):** 1,411 lbs at $25/lb = $35,275
- **Wholesale to specialty shops (40%):** 1,411 lbs at $12/lb = $16,932
- **Local/tourist sales (20%):** 705 lbs at $15/lb = $10,575
- **Total Year 1 Revenue (conservative): ~$62,782**

Low-end scenario (all wholesale, no DTC): 3,527 lbs x $10/lb = $35,270
Conservative blended estimate: **$30,000--$67,500**

### Annual Operating Costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Green coffee purchase (2,000 kg at ~$4.60/lb = $10.14/kg) | $20,280 |
| Packaging and labeling | $2,000--$4,000 |
| Labor (2--3 part-time) | $4,000--$8,000 |
| Energy, transport, maintenance | $2,000--$4,000 |
| Marketing, shipping, export compliance | $2,000--$6,000 |
| **Total Operating Costs** | **$18,000--$40,000** |

Note: If cooperative grows its own coffee, the green bean cost drops dramatically -- the $20,280 represents buying at market. Self-grown cost is more like $3,000--$6,000 for labor and inputs on 5 hectares.

### Net Profit

- Conservative (wholesale-heavy): $30,000 - $40,000 = **-$10,000 to break-even** (Year 1 ramp-up)
- Moderate (blended channels): $50,000 - $30,000 = **$20,000**
- Optimistic (strong DTC): $67,500 - $40,000 = **$27,500**
- **Realistic Year 1 net: $12,000--$27,500** (after ramp-up losses in months 1--4)
- **Break-even: 1.5--3 years** depending on channel mix

### Key Insight

The single biggest lever is selling roasted, not green. Green Bolivian coffee FOB is ~$4.60/lb. Roasted retail Yungas specialty commands $25--45/lb. The roaster is the highest-ROI equipment investment. Self-growing (vs. buying green) is the second biggest lever.

## 2. Cacao Bean-to-Bar Chocolate

### Context

- Bolivia produces fine-flavor cacao (wild Beniano, Alto Beni varieties) -- among the most expensive in South America
- Very small beans (~half normal size), exceptional fruit/floral flavor profile
- Beniano beans command premium pricing but don't fit standard industry machines
- Bolivia has ~15 micro/small chocolate factories domestically
- Global cocoa bean prices: $2.84--$16.30/kg in 2024 (extreme volatility)
- Assumption: 500--2,000 kg beans/year, craft scale

### Investment

| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Small roaster (5--10 kg batch) | $1,000--$5,000 |
| Cracker and winnower | $500--$2,000 |
| Melanger/conche (CocoaTown or similar) | $2,000--$15,000 |
| Tempering machine | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Molds, packaging, labeling | $1,500--$3,000 |
| Facility and storage | $2,000--$5,000 |
| **Total** | **$15,000--$35,000** |

Budget option: CocoaTown starter kit with melanger, roaster, cracker, winnower for ~$5,000.
Production-scale (50+ kg/day): $80,000--$100,000 turnkey systems.

### Revenue Model

**Scenario: 500 kg cacao beans/year (conservative)**

- 500 kg beans yields approximately 300 kg chocolate (accounting for winnowing loss and added sugar/cocoa butter)
- 300 kg chocolate = ~4,285 bars at 70g each
- Retail price: $8--$15 per bar (conservative $8 for wholesale, $12 average blended)

Revenue channels:
- **Tourist/local retail (30%):** 1,286 bars at $12 = $15,432
- **Online DTC (20%):** 857 bars at $12 = $10,284
- **Wholesale to shops (50%):** 2,142 bars at $5 (wholesale) = $10,710
- **Total: ~$36,426**

Conservative (all wholesale at $4/bar): 4,285 x $4 = $17,140
**Conservative range: $17,100--$42,800**

### Annual Operating Costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Cacao beans (500 kg at $8--$12/kg for fine-flavor) | $4,000--$6,000 |
| Sugar, cocoa butter, other ingredients | $1,000--$2,000 |
| Packaging and branding | $1,500--$3,000 |
| Labor (1--2 part-time) | $3,000--$6,000 |
| Energy, transport, maintenance | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Marketing and distribution | $1,000--$4,000 |
| **Total** | **$10,800--$24,000** |

### Net Profit

- Conservative: $17,100 - $24,000 = **-$6,900** (wholesale-only, slow ramp)
- Moderate: $30,000 - $18,000 = **$12,000**
- Optimistic: $42,800 - $24,000 = **$18,800**
- **Realistic Year 1: $6,300--$18,800** (after initial months building channels)
- **Break-even: 2--4 years**

### Key Insight

Bolivian fine-flavor cacao is a luxury product -- the value is in the story and provenance. "Single-origin Bolivian wild cacao, handcrafted in the Yungas" is a compelling narrative for craft chocolate buyers. The highest margins come from direct-to-consumer sales and tourist purchases in Coroico itself. Volume scaling is slow but margins are excellent (60--70% gross on DTC).

## 3. Fruit Solar Dehydration

### Context

- Yungas produces abundant tropical fruit: mango, banana, papaya, passion fruit, citrus
- Solar irradiance is strong at 1,750m elevation
- Solar dehydration has near-zero energy cost -- the sun does the work
- Dried fruit commands 5--10x the price of fresh fruit by weight
- Fresh tropical fruit at farm gate in Bolivia: $0.30--$1.00/kg
- Dried fruit wholesale: $3.50--$22.70/kg (mango); dried banana similar range
- Assumption: 5--10 tons fresh fruit/year

### Investment

| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| DIY solar tunnel dryers (4--6 units) | $200--$1,200 |
| Commercial stainless steel cabinet dryer (1--2 units) | $900--$3,800 |
| Slicing, washing, and prep equipment | $500--$1,500 |
| Packaging (vacuum sealer, bags, labels) | $500--$1,500 |
| Storage facility | $500--$1,000 |
| **Total** | **$2,000--$8,000** |

This is the lowest-investment processing line by far.

### Revenue Model

**Scenario: 5,000 kg (5 tons) fresh fruit processed per year**

- Fresh-to-dried ratio approximately 5:1 to 8:1 (mangos ~7:1, bananas ~5:1)
- 5,000 kg fresh = approximately 700--1,000 kg dried product
- Conservative estimate: 800 kg dried fruit

Revenue channels:
- **Domestic market (40%):** 320 kg at $8/kg = $2,560
- **Export wholesale (40%):** 320 kg at $12/kg = $3,840
- **Tourist/DTC/premium (20%):** 160 kg at $20/kg = $3,200
- **Total: ~$9,600**

Scaling to 10 tons fresh input: ~$19,200
With premium positioning (organic, Yungas-origin): up to $40,000
**Conservative range: $12,500--$40,000** (at 5--10 ton scale)

### Annual Operating Costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Fresh fruit purchase (5,000 kg at $0.50/kg average) | $2,500 |
| Packaging materials | $1,000--$2,000 |
| Labor (2 part-time seasonal) | $2,000--$4,000 |
| Transport and distribution | $500--$2,000 |
| Maintenance and replacement | $200--$500 |
| **Total** | **$5,000--$10,000** |

At 10 tons: $8,000--$15,000

### Net Profit

- Conservative (5 tons, domestic): $12,500 - $10,000 = **$2,500**
- Moderate (7 tons, mixed channels): $20,000 - $12,000 = **$8,000**
- Optimistic (10 tons, premium): $40,000 - $15,000 = **$25,000**
- **Realistic Year 1: $7,500--$25,000**
- **Break-even: 3--12 months** (lowest investment, fastest payback)

### Key Insight

This is the best ROI-to-investment ratio of all five lines. Near-zero energy cost (solar), cheap raw materials (local fruit), and the product has long shelf life and ships easily. The main challenge is quality control (consistent moisture content, food safety certification for export) and establishing distribution channels. Organic certification adds significant price premium.

## 4. Essential Oil Distillation

### Context

- Yungas biodiversity includes eucalyptus, rosemary, and hundreds of native aromatic species
- 200-liter still capacity is a solid small-production setup
- Essential oil industry average profit margins: 40--60% (but ramp-up is slow)
- Time to profitability: typically 2--5 years for new operations
- Eucalyptus and rosemary are low-cost commodity oils -- margin pressure is real
- Higher-value opportunity: unique Yungas botanicals (rare, single-origin, story-driven)
- Assumption: 200--500 liters oil/year

### Investment

| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| 200L stainless steel steam distillation still | $3,000--$7,000 |
| Boiler/heat source | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Condensation system and separator | $500--$1,500 |
| Collection vessels, testing equipment | $500--$2,000 |
| Bottling and labeling equipment | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Initial raw material stock | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Facility setup | $1,000--$2,000 |
| **Total** | **$8,000--$20,000** |

Budget option: A smaller 50--100L copper still for $2,000--$3,600 as proof of concept.

### Revenue Model

**Scenario: 200 liters essential oil/year (conservative)**

Revenue varies enormously by oil type:
- **Eucalyptus oil (commodity):** $15--$30/liter wholesale
- **Rosemary oil:** $30--$60/liter wholesale
- **Premium/rare botanicals:** $80--$300/liter wholesale
- **Retail (10ml bottles):** $15--$50 per bottle = $1,500--$5,000/liter equivalent

Blended scenario (200 liters):
- 100L eucalyptus at $25/L wholesale = $2,500
- 50L rosemary at $45/L wholesale = $2,250
- 30L premium botanicals at $150/L = $4,500
- 20L retail bottled at $2,000/L equivalent = $40,000

Wait -- retail bottling 20L into 10ml bottles = 2,000 bottles at $20 each = $40,000. This changes the math dramatically.

**Conservative (all bulk wholesale, 200L): $6,000--$12,000**
**With 10% retail bottling: $10,000--$25,000**

### Annual Operating Costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Raw plant material (buying, not growing) | $2,000--$5,000 |
| Energy (fuel for boiler) | $500--$1,500 |
| Bottles, labels, packaging | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Labor (1--2 part-time) | $2,000--$4,000 |
| Testing and quality control | $500--$1,000 |
| Transport and marketing | $500--$1,500 |
| **Total** | **$4,000--$12,000** |

Growing your own aromatics (eucalyptus groves, rosemary beds, native botanical gardens) drops raw material cost to near zero after establishment.

### Net Profit

- Conservative (bulk wholesale): $6,000 - $12,000 = **-$6,000** (loss Year 1)
- Moderate (mixed wholesale + some retail): $15,000 - $8,000 = **$7,000**
- Optimistic (strong retail channel): $25,000 - $12,000 = **$13,000**
- **Realistic Year 1: $2,000--$13,000** (ramp-up is slow for this line)
- **Break-even: 2--5 years**

### Key Insight

Commodity oils (eucalyptus, rosemary) have thin margins at small scale -- large producers in China and India dominate on price. The real opportunity is: (a) unique Yungas botanicals that no one else can produce, and (b) retail-bottled "single-origin Bolivian essential oils" sold DTC for $15--$50 per 10ml bottle. The wholesale-to-retail price multiplier is 50--100x. This line lives or dies on the retail/DTC channel.

**IMPORTANT CAVEAT:** The search data found that "for large scale production of low cost oils such as rosemary, eucalyptus... the world market prices of the oils are barely high enough to allow them to be produced by steam distillation without amortizing the capital expenditure over 10 years." This line should prioritize unique botanicals over commodity oils.

## 5. Solar Installation Services

### Context

- Bolivia committed $325 million to rural solar electrification for 140,000 families
- World Bank approved additional $125 million for rural electric access
- Target: universal electricity access by 2030
- Typical rural solar home system: 300W capacity (basic lighting + appliance charging)
- Government project calculates ~$16,250 per household at program scale
- Typical residential solar installation revenue: $10,000--$30,000 per project (US data; Bolivia lower)
- Solar installer profit margins: 20--40% gross, 10--25% net
- Assumption: 50--100 installations/year serving rural Yungas communities

### Investment

| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Tools and test equipment (multimeters, wire strippers, crimpers, etc.) | $2,000--$5,000 |
| Vehicle for transport to rural sites | $5,000--$15,000 |
| Training and certification (team of 3--5 technicians) | $3,000--$8,000 |
| Initial inventory (panels, inverters, batteries, mounting hardware) | $5,000--$15,000 |
| Business setup, insurance, licensing | $2,000--$5,000 |
| **Total** | **$15,000--$40,000** |

Note: Under the government program, equipment is likely procured/supplied separately. The installer's value is labor, expertise, and local presence.

### Revenue Model

**Scenario: 50 installations/year (conservative), rural off-grid systems**

Bolivia context pricing (not US pricing):
- Basic solar home system installation service: $500--$1,500 per installation (labor + markup)
- Larger community/school systems: $2,000--$5,000 per project
- Maintenance contracts: $50--$100/year per installation

Revenue:
- **Basic installations (40):** 40 x $1,000 = $40,000
- **Larger systems (10):** 10 x $3,000 = $30,000
- **Maintenance contracts (20):** 20 x $75 = $1,500
- **Total Year 1: ~$71,500**

Conservative (50 basic installs only): 50 x $1,000 = $50,000
Optimistic (100 mixed installs): $150,000
**Conservative range: $50,000--$150,000**

### Annual Operating Costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Technician wages (3--5 people) | $12,000--$30,000 |
| Vehicle fuel and maintenance | $3,000--$8,000 |
| Equipment and materials (pass-through, if not government-supplied) | $15,000--$50,000 |
| Insurance and compliance | $1,000--$3,000 |
| Admin, communications, marketing | $2,000--$5,000 |
| Training updates and certifications | $1,000--$3,000 |
| **Total** | **$35,000--$105,000** |

### Net Profit

- Conservative (50 basic, low margin): $50,000 - $50,000 = **break-even**
- Moderate (50 mixed, established): $71,500 - $50,000 = **$21,500**
- Optimistic (100 installs, maintenance base): $150,000 - $105,000 = **$45,000**
- **Realistic Year 1: $15,000--$45,000**
- **Break-even: 1--2 years**

### Key Insight

This is the most "certain" revenue stream because demand is government-created and funded. The $325M program creates guaranteed demand for tens of thousands of installations. The competitive advantage is local presence in the Yungas -- international contractors won't bother with remote mountain communities. BARK-trained technicians who know the terrain, speak the language, and can maintain the systems long-term are exactly what the program needs.

The maintenance revenue is the long-term play: once you've installed 200+ systems, the annual maintenance income becomes a stable recurring revenue base.

## Combined Portfolio Analysis

### If BARK invests in all five lines simultaneously:

| Metric | Conservative | Moderate | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total Investment | $65,000 | $110,000 | $158,000 |
| Year 1 Revenue | $116,100 | $192,000 | $325,300 |
| Year 1 Costs | $72,800 | $118,000 | $196,000 |
| Year 1 Net Profit | $43,300 | $74,000 | $129,300 |
| Portfolio Break-Even | ~18 months | ~15 months | ~12 months |

### Priority Ranking (by ROI speed):

1. **Fruit Solar Dehydration** -- Fastest payback (3--12 months), lowest investment, highest certainty
2. **Solar Installation Services** -- Government-funded demand, fastest revenue ramp, recurring maintenance income
3. **Coffee Processing** -- Highest absolute margins, strong brand potential, but needs channel development
4. **Cacao Bean-to-Bar** -- Premium product with great narrative, but slower scaling
5. **Essential Oil Distillation** -- Best when focused on unique botanicals + retail; commodity oils are a trap

### Critical Success Factors

1. **Channel development matters more than production.** All five lines can produce product. The bottleneck is always getting it to paying customers. DTC/online sales, export relationships, and tourist foot traffic are the three channels that need deliberate investment.

2. **Self-growing vs. buying raw materials** changes the economics dramatically for coffee, cacao, and aromatics. A cooperative that grows its own inputs has 30--50% lower operating costs.

3. **Organic and fair-trade certification** adds $2--5/lb for coffee and premium pricing for all lines. Worth the paperwork investment.

4. **The Techno Park's shared equipment model** reduces per-venture capital requirements. Not every coffee startup needs its own roaster -- a shared community roaster serves multiple producers.
